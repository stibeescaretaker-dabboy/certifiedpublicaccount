Skin Graphs (CGI) ((Working Title))

Toner On Vellum, 11" x 8.5"

CGI as a tool to generate a hyperreality that fits expectations of grandiose spectacles, explosions, natural disasters, astral phenomena, to reinforce a contrived perception informed by cinema. Galvan digitally simulates existential phenomena, to capture unfeasible images. The gravitas assumed by the images are trivialized by being printed on malfunctioning laserjet printers and re scanned. They par these images with surreal depictions of flesh with its layers between the epidermis peeled, where melanin is concentrated but not limited too.

The Pink Face Beyond The Magnifying GlassPastel on Canvas, 30"x20"

Once when visiting my Abuelo and Umi, in Hempstead, I found there magnifying lens. I went outside to use it and the sun to burn some ants. I was no more than six, this was not my first time considering this activity. But this time I commited. I felt like I had superpowers, I felt like a scientist, the guilt I felt came creeped in slowly. I cried. I bigged the ants for forgiveness.

"My Mother Told Me A Man Created Jazz"InkJet on Celluse Paper, 72" x 36"

Digitized notebook drawing collaged into narratives pertaining to the historical acts of erasure, accrued by the disenfranchised. In doing so I catalog cultural motifs and Iconography, particularly those that have fallen subject to speculation, a need to rediscover the story and meanings from archeological clues often bestowed by a western practitioner, a continuation of erasure. This collection of icons serves as a lexicon that subverts the approach of the historical practice of art as a means of storytelling, tracing back to when stained glass was more accessible than the bible for the illiterate. Displacing icons from their historical context and original narrative, I digitally collage them to invent new convoluted and abstract narratives. These narratives parallel my own experiences of being disconnected from parts of my family heritage.

"Spilled from The Guts of The Migrant Blue Bird… / The Proper People plucked em , plumped em, n' popped em / At its Final Rest The Blue Bird is cooked by the warmth of applause / While the chickadees bathe in the stench" Part III.InkJet on Celluse Paper, 72" x 36"

My abuelo (Spanish for grandfather) was listed as a "bastardo" on his birth certificate. Half indigenous Quechuan and half Spaniard, he was disowned and homeless, but latched on to the tip of academia by gaining solace from his love of Roman philosophy and Western literature. I loved my abuelo... but he left behind a "stench" - a mystery of untold stories, his cultural heritage and his lineage - despite becoming an academic with 4 PhDs. This new work will examine my relationship with his immigrant story (from Peru to queens), the privileges his sacrifice has allotting me and the past that he suppressed.
