Skin Graphs (CGI) ((Working Title))

Toner On Vellum, 11" x 8.5"

CGI as a tool to generate a hyperreality that fits expectations of grandiose spectacles, explosions, natural disasters, astral phenomena, to reinforce a contrived perception informed by cinema. Galvan digitally simulates existential phenomena, to capture unfeasible images. The gravitas assumed by the images are trivialized by being printed on malfunctioning laserjet printers and re scanned. They par these images with surreal depictions of flesh with its layers between the epidermis peeled, where melanin is concentrated but not limited too.

Artist Statement Draft 3.03 (too long):

Through printmaking techniques and paint application, Galvan constructs images of a liminal landscape — a field caught in limbo. From shifting vantage points, they render moments from a singular environment. Their process starts with scaled down studies on vellum paper, a material used for architectural drafting. Galvan incorporates materials and software used towards urban development as a foundation for their works, tracing how systems of representation, construction, and surveillance shape our understanding of landscape. Their palette draws from the shifting values of CMYK inks used in commercial printing, to align with film stocks used for aerial surveillance, urban planning, and military operations. This connection extends their interest in extrapolating images from their underlying mechanisms. The paintings are composed of elongated halftones, enlarging the negligibly small marks that viewers most often encounter through printed images until they become the dominant visual structure. Stretched beyond their original function, these dots become lines that gesture toward the physicality of a paint stroke.

Paint is applied in monotonous, repetitive, and thick patches, imparting the surfaces with a textile sensibility. By stretching the relatively instantaneous process of printing halftones across the duration of painting, Galvan transforms a mechanism of reproduction into a means of reflection. The paintings become records of both an image and the labor required to reconstruct its underlying systems.

The surfaces of their work are “whitewashed,” through repeated layers of heavily thinned lead white paint applied over transparent gesso. This process produces hazy gradients between paint and substrate, while the varying hues beneath the white draw attention to whiteness as an active intrusion into the surface rather than a neutral or “pure” material state. The residual presence of lead within domestic structures introduces another material tension, using the familiar and potentially hazardous substance to evoke a sense of the uncanny or unheimlichkeit. Galvan often builds the frames for their work from poplar wood. The wood serves as frame, scaffolding, or window, extending the paintings’ investigation of structures through which landscapes are constructed, viewed, and understood. The tree’s historical context as a site of lynching introduces a history of racialized violence into this structural vocabulary, providing a lens through which Galvan approaches the work as a means of coming to terms with suffering and grief.

Their work has been significant in forming an understanding on displacement. They turn to their familial history as a dichotomy between voluntary and involuntary uprooting, to exercise trauma converging from their black, hispanic, european, and indigenous ties. They use phenomena of fish being caught and change in pressure causing deformation and expansion of vestibular organs, as a bridge between displacement and their personal chronic pain within their inner ear. Animals within their work often echo this experience through a process of contortion, with elements of their bodies stretched according to mapping points positioned at a distance from a binding coordinate. This invisible coordinate functions as a theoretical site of multipositioned existence: a condition in which the soul and body are simultaneously situated apart from one another. The resulting distortions consider displacement as both a physical and psychological condition, where the body can remain present while the self is divided across multiple positions.

The Pink Face Beyond The Magnifying GlassPastel on Canvas, 30"x20"

Once when visiting my Abuelo and Umi, in Hempstead, I found there magnifying lens. I went outside to use it and the sun to burn some ants. "My Mother Told Me A Man Created Jazz"InkJet on Celluse Paper, 72" x 36"

Digitized notebook drawing collaged into narratives pertaining to the historical acts of erasure, accrued by the disenfranchised. In doing so I catalog cultural motifs and Iconography, particularly those that have fallen subject to speculation, a need to rediscover the story and meanings from archeological clues often bestowed by a western practitioner, a continuation of erasure. This collection of icons serves as a lexicon that subverts the approach of the historical practice of art as a means of storytelling, tracing back to when stained glass was more accessible than the bible for the illiterate. Displacing icons from their historical context and original narrative, I digitally collage them to invent new convoluted and abstract narratives. These narratives parallel my own experiences of being disconnected from parts of my family heritage.

"Spilled from The Guts of The Migrant Blue Bird… / The Proper People plucked em , plumped em, n' popped em / At its Final Rest The Blue Bird is cooked by the warmth of applause / While the chickadees bathe in the stench" Part III.InkJet on Celluse Paper, 72" x 36"

My abuelo (Spanish for grandfather) was listed as a "bastardo" on his birth certificate. Half indigenous Quechuan and half Spaniard, he was disowned and homeless, but latched on to the tip of academia by gaining solace from his love of Roman philosophy and Western literature. I loved my abuelo... but he left behind a "stench" - a mystery of untold stories, his cultural heritage and his lineage - despite becoming an academic with 4 PhDs. This new work will examine my relationship with his immigrant story (from Peru to queens), the privileges his sacrifice has allotting me and the past that he suppressed.
